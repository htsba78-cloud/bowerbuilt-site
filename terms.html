Tatum is an AI, and it can be wrong

Tatum's recommendations, bottle identifications and tasting notes are generated by an AI model. They are suggestions, not facts. In particular:

  • Tatum can misidentify a bottle. When you scan or add a bottle, the result may be wrong. Always read the actual label before you pour.
  • Tatum does not know what you're allergic to, and may suggest ingredients containing allergens, additives or animal products. If you have an allergy or dietary restriction, check every ingredient yourself.
  • Tatum may be wrong about alcohol content. Strength varies by product and by how a drink is made. Don't rely on Tatum to judge how strong anything is.
  • Tatum is not a source of medical, dietary or nutritional advice and nothing it says should be treated as such. If alcohol interacts with a medication or a condition you have, talk to a doctor or pharmacist, not a cocktail app.

Drinking responsibly

Tatum suggests drinks. It has no idea how many you've had, what you weigh, what you've eaten, what you've taken, or whether you're about to drive. All of that is on you.

Never drink and drive. Don't serve alcohol to anyone under the legal drinking age or to anyone who is already intoxicated. Know your limits and stay well inside them.
